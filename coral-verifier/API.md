# Coral verifier service: HTTP API v1

The contract between the Coral verifier service (HA add-on, owns the Coral USB, runs YOLOv9-s int8 512) and its clients (the Scrypted mixin plugin, optionally Home Assistant). Both sides are built against this file. Change it here first.

Principles: the service is a sidecar and never in the video path. It is stateless for clients except per-camera sessions that expire by themselves. Clients must fail open: if the service is down, slow or returns an error, the client behaves exactly as it does without the service.

## Transport and auth

HTTP/1.1, JSON bodies, UTF-8. Default port 8750, bound on all interfaces of the host network. The add-on option `token` is REQUIRED (the service refuses to start with an empty one): every request except `GET /v1/health` must carry `X-Verifier-Token: <token>`; it is checked BEFORE any body is read (401, connection closed). Request limits: bodies need a `Content-Length` (chunked is refused with 411), at most 2 MB (`413`), headers and body must each arrive within 5 s in total (slow clients are cut), at most 64 concurrent connections (extra ones get an immediate 503). Timestamps are unix milliseconds. Boxes are normalised `[x0, y0, x1, y1]` in 0..1 of the submitted frame.

## Endpoints

`GET /v1/health` returns `{ok, version, model:{name,input,coral:"ok|stalled|missing"}, coral_ms:{median,p95,last}, stages:{decode_ms|preprocess_ms|infer_ms|post_ms|queue_ms|total_ms: {median,p95}}, dropped_stale, resets, queue_depth, cameras_active, uptime_s}`. `coral_ms` is the invoke time (same as `infer_ms`), `stages` are percentiles over the last 200 frames, `dropped_stale` counts frames shed or dropped as stale since start. `ok` is false when the Coral is missing or stalled; the service keeps retrying in the background. In that case the response status is HTTP 503 (the body is the same JSON with `ok:false`): clients and the Supervisor watchdog treat a non-200 health as unhealthy. Health also reports `t_clamped`, the number of frames whose `t` was replaced by server time (see rule 10).

`POST /v1/cameras/{camera}/frame` with body = one JPEG (`Content-Type: image/jpeg`; JPEG ONLY: the body must start with FFD8 and its frame header is read BEFORE decoding, anything else or above the pixel cap, default 16 MP, is `400`; 640 px wide or more recommended), query `t=<unix_ms>` (capture time, default now), optional `tiles=auto|1|2|3` (default auto: width/height <= 2.2 is one pass; wider frames run HYBRID = the WHOLE frame letterboxed plus two overlapping 55 % half tiles, 3 Coral invokes per frame, whole-frame detections win over tile pieces: a tile detection is dropped when > 60 % of it lies inside a same-group whole-frame detection, or when it is taller than 25 % of the frame height and overlaps one (IoU > 0.3), and the inner-tile-edge rule below applies only to tile detections; merged with per-class NMS (IoU 0.5) plus the vehicle cross-class NMS; whole frame is still ONE request/queue slot, `infer_ms`/`coral_ms` are summed over the invokes; explicit `1` = whole frame only, `2` = the two 55 % tiles only, `3` = three evenly spread tiles with 30 % overlap and no whole pass; the whole frame is still ONE request/queue slot with several Coral invokes inside, `infer_ms`/`coral_ms` are summed over them. Constants: `defaults.TILING`), optional `deadline_ms=<n>` (> 0, otherwise 400; the longest this frame may wait in the service queue; older frames are dropped with `503 {reason:"stale"}`), optional `prio=normal|low` (default normal, otherwise 400; `low` marks background monitoring frames: when the queue is full the oldest LOW frame is shed first, and a low frame is only served when no normal frame is waiting, so it never delays a normal one), optional `verbose=1` (include detections below 0.2; by default replies omit them). The service letterboxes, runs the Coral, updates that camera's session and replies:

  "t": 1791886000000,
  "coral_ms": 31.2, "queue_ms": 4.0,

Detection classes and thresholds (internal constants in `verifier/defaults.py`, not add-on options). Every detection is `{cls, group, score, box}`, `group` = `person|vehicle|animal`. airplane, train, boat and elephant are never reported.

| group | classes | reported / tracked from | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| person | person | camera `cont_thr` (default 0.2) | state machine below, unchanged |
| vehicle | bicycle, car, motorcycle, bus, truck | car, motorcycle, bus, truck 0.40, bicycle 0.5 (car, motorcycle, bus, truck also reported weak in [0.25, 0.40)) | tracking and parked logic below; a bicycle behaves exactly like a car: it makes `vehicle.moving` true while non-parked and `parked` after `parked_after_s` |
| animal | bird, cat, dog, horse, sheep, cow, bear | 0.3 (confirmation 0.6) | `animal` object below |

`animal`: an independent state machine with the person tick logic (same 1-second ticks and merge of frames < 0.75 s apart) on animal detections of any class: confirmed when a detection >= 0.6 is seen in 2 of 3 one-second ticks; kept alive while detections >= 0.3 matched to a track keep arriving; held 6 s after the last one (no stationary lock). `present` = `confirmed` = confirmed or holding (both fields carry the same value). `tracks` are simple IoU tracks (`id`, `cls`, `since_ms` = first seen) that live for the 6 s hold and exist only while `present`; there is no parked concept. Constants: `defaults.ANIMAL`. Multi-tile frames: a box within 2 % of the tile width of an INNER tile edge (not the frame border) whose extent lies entirely inside the neighbouring tile is dropped (the neighbour sees the object whole); boxes touching only the frame border are kept. After the tile merge, car/truck/bus/motorcycle go through one cross-class NMS (IoU 0.5, higher score wins and keeps its class) and a vehicle box with more than 70 % of its area inside a larger vehicle box is dropped. Bicycle and the other groups are not part of it. Weak vehicles: car, motorcycle, bus and truck detections with score in [0.25, 0.40) are listed in `detections` with `"weak": true` (group `vehicle`) for display only (bicycle has no weak band); strong ones (>= 0.40) carry no `weak` field. Weak vehicles never feed vehicle tracks, `parked`, `vehicle.moving` or any session state; per-class NMS ranks by score so a weak box cannot suppress a strong one. Animals never affect the person state or the vehicle object (separate trackers, enforced by tests). Reading `GET /v1/cameras/{camera}` includes `animal`.

Person state machine (per camera, internal defaults, overridable per camera): `idle` (no track), `candidate` (a detection >= `start_thr` seen but not yet confirmed), `confirmed` (>= `confirm_n` TICKS with a box >= `start_thr` inside `confirm_m` seconds; default 2 of 3, start_thr 0.65; a tick is one second of frame time, frames less than 0.75 s apart are the SAME tick and merge, best score wins), `holding` (confirmed, no box >= `cont_thr` this frame, still within `hold_s`; default 8 s). While confirmed or holding, boxes >= `cont_thr` (default 0.2) matched to an existing track (IoU or centre-distance gate) keep it alive. `present` = state is `confirmed` or `holding`. A track that has not matched for `hold_s` ends; a track whose box has IoU > 0.7 with its own box `still_s` seconds earlier (default 3 s, rolling, as in the 1 fps reference simulation) is a "stationary lock" and survives gaps up to `still_hold_s` (default 30 s). `max_hold_s` (default 600 s) is a service-side safety with no counterpart in the reference simulation: a track without a detection >= `start_thr` for that long ends. The person logic reproduces the reference simulation's simulation decision for decision at 1 fps (tests/test_tracker_diff.py), including its off-by-one: a track may still be re-matched when its gap was `hold_s` + 1 s. Vehicles: boxes of car/truck/bus/motorcycle >= 0.40 and bicycle >= 0.5; a track with IoU > 0.7 between consecutive frames for > `parked_after_s` (default 120 s) is `parked`; `vehicle.moving` is true when a non-parked vehicle track is present.

`POST /v1/cameras/{camera}/config` with any of `{start_thr, cont_thr, confirm_n, confirm_m, hold_s, still_s, still_hold_s, max_hold_s, parked_after_s, session_ttl_s, vehicle_thr, animal_start_thr, animal_cont_thr, zoom_out, second_look}` sets per-camera overrides (200 with the effective config). `vehicle_thr` (default 0.40, 0.1-0.95) is the strong floor of car/truck/bus/motorcycle (bicycle stays 0.5); the weak display band is [0.25, vehicle_thr) and exists only when `vehicle_thr` > 0.25. `animal_start_thr` (default 0.6) and `animal_cont_thr` (default 0.3, also the animal decode floor) must satisfy 0.05 <= cont <= start <= 0.95. Person keeps `start_thr`/`cont_thr`. `zoom_out` (default 0 = off, 0..0.6): when > 0 the frame job adds ONE extra Coral pass with the whole frame shrunk to (1 - zoom_out) of its size, centred on a grey (114) canvas of the original size and letterboxed as usual (helps large objects cut by the frame edge); its boxes are mapped back to the original frame's normalised coordinates (scale and offset undone, clamped to 0..1) and merged like whole-frame boxes (per-class NMS plus vehicle cross-class NMS, exempt from the inner-tile-edge rule); stage timings sum over passes, still one request/queue slot. These affect decoding floors, filtering and trackers of THAT camera only (the engine decodes each frame with that camera's floors). `GET` on the same path returns it.

`POST /v1/cameras/{camera}/reset` clears the session (call on a raw-motion rising edge when a fresh episode starts). `GET /v1/cameras/{camera}` returns the current session state without submitting a frame (same shape as the frame reply minus detections); hold and vehicle expiry advance with the SERVER clock measured from the last frame's receipt, so a state read after silence shows the expired state. `GET /v1/cameras` lists active sessions. Sessions with no frame for `session_ttl_s` (default 120) are dropped.

`GET /v1/metrics` (token required, `X-Verifier-Token`) is the performance page feed. It returns `{coral:{state, invoke_ms:{median,p95,last}, utilization_pct, invocations_per_s, resets}, stages:{decode_ms|preprocess_ms|infer_ms|post_ms|queue_ms|total_ms:{median,p95}}, load:{queue_depth, dropped_stale_total, dropped_stale_60s, rejected_429_60s, cameras_active, frames_60s}, host:{cpu_pct, load1, mem_used_pct}, process:{cpu_pct, rss_mb}, gpu:{available, busy_pct}, uptime_s}`. It always answers 200 (also while the Coral is stalled: read `coral.state`). `utilization_pct` is the share of the last 60 s (or of the uptime when shorter) the Coral spent in invokes; `invocations_per_s`, `dropped_stale_60s`, `rejected_429_60s` (rate limit and camera cap) and `frames_60s` (frames answered 200) are over the same window, kept in a per-second ring (no per-frame logging). `host.cpu_pct` is system-wide and `gpu.busy_pct` is 100 minus the rc6 residency share of the first `/sys/class/drm/card*/gt/gt0/rc6_residency_ms`; both, and `process.cpu_pct` (this service plus the Coral child, 100 = one core, may exceed 100), come from a background sampler that runs every 5 s, so they cover the last ~5 s and are null for the first one. `gpu.available` is false (and `busy_pct` null) when the sysfs file is unreadable, for instance inside a container. Any value that cannot be read is `null`; the endpoint never fails because of it.

## Contract rulings (v1, binding)

1. Frame times: frames for one camera may carry `t` in the past (a pre-roll burst) and `t` must be non-decreasing per camera; an out-of-order `t` is clamped to the last one. All session timing (`since_ms`, `hold_s`, `confirm_m`, `still_s`) uses frame time. Pre-roll must be sent SEQUENTIALLY (wait for each reply): a deep parallel burst makes its oldest frames stale by design.
2. Rate limit: a burst of up to 10 frames within 1 s per camera is accepted (pre-roll); the 429 limit of 4 fps per camera applies to the sustained rate (token bucket, 10 capacity, 4 per second refill).
3. `reset` takes effect before any later request on the same connection and never clears per-camera config overrides; it also clears vehicle tracks. Clients wait for the 200 before sending frames.
4. `person.confirmed` is true for the whole time the state is `confirmed` or `holding` (sticky for the episode, false in `idle` and `candidate`); `present` is the same today but is the field clients should use for "someone is there".
5. `GET /v1/cameras/{camera}` and `GET /v1/cameras/{camera}/config` for an unknown camera return 404 with `{}`; `POST .../config` and `POST .../frame` create it. A camera is known while it has a live session or a config override.
6. `GET /v1/health` never returns 401.
7. `vehicle.moving` is true only when a vehicle track is in state `moving`. Each track has `state` = `moving|still|parked` (`parked` bool kept for compatibility). A new track is `still` (unknown, NOT moving): it becomes `moving` when, against its box at least 2 s of frame time earlier, the centre moved more than 3 % of the normalised frame diagonal or the IoU fell below 0.7, and `parked` after `parked_after_s` (120 s) without such movement (IoU > 0.7 with its anchor). A car that stops returns to `still` 2 s after it last moved. A track that vanishes keeps its last state for up to `hold_s`. A parked car seen after a service restart is therefore never `moving`. Constants: `defaults.MOVE_*`.
8. `max_hold_s` (service) is the longest a person may be held WITHOUT a confident detection (>= `start_thr`); the plugin has its own "Max hold" measured from confirmation.
9. `GET /v1/cameras` returns `{"cameras":[{camera, t, person_state, present, vehicle_moving, frames, age_s}]}`. `POST .../reset` returns `{camera, reset:true, had_session}`. Vehicle `since_ms` is the time the track was first seen.
10. Frame time bound: `t` is trusted only inside [server now - 60 s, server now + 5 s] and must be finite; otherwise (also 0, negative, inf, nan) the server receive time is used and `t_clamped` in health is incremented. A single bad `t` can never freeze or poison a session. A `t` that is not a number at all is `400`.
11. At most 64 cameras are known at once (option `max_cameras`); a new camera name beyond that is `429 {"error":"too many cameras"}` on frame and config. Camera names match `[A-Za-z0-9_.-]{1,64}`. Idle config overrides are dropped after 7 days.
12. `GET /v1/metrics` requires the token (401 otherwise), always returns 200 with the shape above, never raises on unreadable `/proc` or `/sys` (those fields are `null`, `gpu.available` false), and its 60 s figures are cheap rolling counters, not logs. `GET /v1/health` keeps all its fields unchanged.

## Latency policy (v1, binding)

The service never builds a backlog: the shared queue is bounded (internal `max_queue_depth` 8; when full the OLDEST request is shed) and a frame whose age since REQUEST ARRIVAL (decode included) exceeds `max_queue_age_ms` (default 400) or its own `deadline_ms` when it reaches the Coral is dropped. Both answer `503 {"reason":"stale"}`: clients treat this as "skip this frame", NOT as an outage (the Coral is fine, `/v1/health` stays ok). Stage timings: `decode_ms` JPEG decode (optionally at a reduced size, add-on option `jpeg_reduce`, OFF by default), `preprocess_ms` crop + letterbox + RGB + quantisation, `infer_ms` set_tensor + invoke + get_tensor (summed over tiles), `post_ms` decode + NMS + tile merge, `queue_ms` wait before the worker, `total_ms` arrival through post (excludes the network and JSON encode). Replies are compact, TCP_NODELAY and keep-alive are on, nothing is logged per frame at info level.

## Errors and limits

`400` bad image / bad parameter (messages never contain internals), `401` bad token, `411` Content-Length required (chunked refused), `413` body larger than 2 MB, `429` too many frames from one camera (sustained 4 per second, burst 10, body `{retry_after_ms}`), `503` Coral unavailable (body `{coral:"stalled|missing"}`) or frame dropped as stale (body `{reason:"stale"}`, see Latency policy), `500` otherwise. Clients treat any non-200 or a request taking longer than 2 s as "service unavailable" and fail open. Frames are processed in order of ARRIVAL (measured when the request is received, before decoding) through one Coral queue shared by all cameras; the Coral is owned by a child process that the service kills and restarts when an invoke takes more than 2 s or fails; the service never lets a stalled Coral block the HTTP server.

## Optional Home Assistant surface (phase 2)

MQTT discovery `binary_sensor.<camera>_verified_person`, `binary_sensor.<camera>_verified_vehicle`, `sensor.<camera>_verifier_state`, published from the same sessions. Not needed for the Scrypted plugin.

Second look (per-camera config key `second_look`, bool, default true when never set; the plugin sends the same value for every camera; max 2 re-checks per frame and the other limits are internal, `defaults.SECOND_LOOK`): inside the same frame job, after the normal passes and the merge, uncertain detections are re-checked on a full-resolution crop. Candidates are detections in the borderline band of their group (person `cont_thr` <= score < `start_thr`; vehicle weak or strong floor <= score < 0.6; animal `animal_cont_thr` <= score < `animal_start_thr`) plus any detection that starts a NEW track (no live track of its group overlaps it) with score < 0.8. At most 2 candidates per frame are re-checked, the ones closest to their confirm threshold first. Each re-check takes a window of the decoded frame ALWAYS centred on the box and expanded by 0.6 of the box size on each side (square, at least 96 px), not shifted or clamped inside the frame: the part of the window outside the frame is filled grey (114), so the margin stays symmetric and a large box gets a window bigger than the frame with the object shrunk inside grey; the window is letterboxed to 512 and runs ONE Coral pass for that group's classes; the best same-group detection whose centre lies inside the original box wins. Reply detections then carry `second_look`: `confirmed` (score kept or raised) or `lowered`, with `first_score` holding the original score and `score` the second-look one (a vehicle's `weak` flag follows the new score), `not_found` when nothing of that group was found in the window (the detection is kept exactly as it was and still feeds the trackers, so half-occluded parked cars are not lost), `not_upgraded` (found again but below the group's keep floor: person `cont_thr`, vehicle strong floor, animal `animal_cont_thr`; the detection is kept unchanged), `lowered` (found at or above the keep floor but below the first score: the new score is used), or `rejected` only on a confident downgrade: the same group was found again with a score below 0.5 x that keep floor (`SECOND_LOOK['reject_factor']`; the re-check decodes down to that level) while the first score was below the confirm threshold. A rejected detection never reaches the trackers (`first_score`/`score` hold the first and re-check scores) and is listed only with `verbose=1`. A re-checked object is not looked at again for 10 s: later frames reuse its result by box overlap with no extra invoke (the cache is cleared by `reset`). The frame stays ONE request and queue slot: expect 0 to 2 extra Coral invokes per frame (usually 0), summed into `infer_ms`; the replies carry `second_look_ms` (wall time of the re-checks, 0 when none). `GET /v1/metrics` has `second_look: {count_60s, reused_60s, confirmed_share, lowered_share, rejected_share, not_found_share, not_upgraded_share}` and `stages.second_look_ms`. `second_look: false` in `POST /v1/cameras/{camera}/config` turns it off for that camera (400 when not a boolean).

## Event journal (0.3.0)

`POST /v1/cameras/{camera}/episodes` (token) with `{id, action: "start"|"update"|"end", t_ms, mode?: "shadow"|"gate", kind?: "camera"|"unflagged", outcome?: "confirmed"|"vetoed"|"aborted"|"released"|"parked", groups?: ["person", ...], note?, timeline_event?: {t_ms, text}}`. `id` is a client-chosen unique id (6-64 characters of `A-Za-z0-9_-`, e.g. a uuid; anything else is 400, so ids are safe as folder names). `start` creates the episode (repeating it is idempotent; defaults `mode` shadow, `kind` camera), `update` merges `groups`/`note`/`mode`/`kind`/an `outcome` and appends a `timeline_event`, `end` closes it with `outcome` (default `released`). The body must be sent as `Content-Type: application/json` (415 otherwise, also for the label route). Client times (`t_ms`, `timeline_event.t_ms`) are clamped to server time +- 1 day. At most 64 ongoing episodes in total and 8 per camera: a `start` beyond that is `429`. Replies `200 {id, status: "ongoing"|"closed", outcome}`; 400 invalid, 404 unknown id, 409 already ended or the id belongs to another camera, 429 too many ongoing episodes, 503 journal unavailable or busy. Every frame answered for that camera between `start` and `end` is linked to the episode; on `start` the frames received up to 5 s before are adopted (the trigger frame). The service keeps the last 6 frames per camera in memory, so first, best and last are picked without disk churn: `first` is written at once, `best` (highest score of the episode's `groups`, any group when empty; second-look rejected detections do not count) and `last` are written on `update`, `end`, stale close or when the episode is read, plus one `snapshot` per 600 s of frame time while ongoing (at most 12). JPEGs are the original bytes as received (no re-encode); each has a sidecar `<name>.json` `{t_ms, detections: [{cls, group, score, box, weak?, masked?, first_score?, second_look?}]}` (all detections of that frame, rejected ones included). An ongoing episode without any update or frame for 2 h is closed as `stale`. Closed episodes older than 14 days are deleted, and when the journal exceeds 2 GB the oldest closed ones go first (constants in `defaults.JOURNAL`; the journal lives in `/data/journal`, so it is part of Home Assistant backups; `journal_root` is overridable for tests only).

Layout: `<root>/<id>/meta.json` `{id, camera, start_ms, end_ms, mode, kind, outcome, groups, note, timeline: [{t_ms, text}], label, label_note, labels: [history], frames: [{name, role: first|best|last|snapshot, t_ms}], status, last_activity_ms}`, `first.jpg/.json`, `best.*`, `last.*`, `s01.*` ...

Review API (token, or the ingress rule below):
- `GET /v1/journal?camera=&outcome=&label=&from=&to=&limit=&offset=` returns `{total, offset, items: [meta]}` newest first (`from`/`to` are epoch ms on the episode start, `label=none` = unlabeled, `limit` max 200, default 50).
- `GET /v1/journal/{id}` returns the meta (an ongoing episode is flushed first). `GET /v1/journal/{id}/{name}.jpg` the stored JPEG, `GET /v1/journal/{id}/{name}.json` its sidecar. 404 for anything else.
- `POST /v1/journal/{id}/label` with `{label: "correct"|"false_alarm"|"missed"|"wrong_class", note?}` persists the label in meta.json (`labels` keeps the history).
- `GET /v1/journal/summary` returns `{rows: [{camera, group, total, by_outcome: {...}, by_label: {..., unlabeled}}]}` (an episode counts for each of its groups, `none` when it has none).
- `GET /v1/metrics` has `journal: {enabled, episodes, ongoing, bytes, queue, dropped}`. All journal writes run on one worker thread behind a bounded queue (256): the frame path only enqueues and never waits, and frame work is dropped (counted in `dropped`) when the queue is full; episode and label calls wait for the worker (503 busy when the queue is full). `limit`, `offset`, `from` and `to` must be finite numbers (400 otherwise).

Review page: `GET /` (also `/ui`) serves a single static page (vanilla HTML/JS/CSS, no external resources, relative URLs, emulating the Home Assistant look: HA variable names and default palette, app bar with the shield mark and a tab bar, light/dark and mobile friendly; the add-on store images are `icon.png` and `logo.png`): filter bar, event cards with first/best/last thumbnails and boxes drawn from the sidecars, a detail view with frames, timeline, detections and label buttons, and a summary tab with the accuracy table per camera and group. The add-on declares `ingress: true`, `ingress_port: 8750`, `panel_icon: mdi:shield-search`, `panel_title: Coral Guard`, so it shows in the Home Assistant sidebar. Authentication: a request coming from the Supervisor ingress gateway (source 172.30.32.2 AND an `X-Ingress-Path` header) needs no token, but only for the `/v1/journal` routes; everything else, and any request from elsewhere, still needs `X-Verifier-Token`. The page itself carries no data.

## Session snapshot additions (0.3.0, backward compatible)

`person.tracks_detail`: `[{id, box, score, last_seen_ms, since_ms, stationary}]` for the live tracks (`person.tracks` stays the count; `score` is the last matched detection score, `last_seen_ms` the tick time of the last match). `animal.tracks[]` also carry `last_seen_ms` and `box`. `person`, `vehicle` and `animal` each carry `hold_until_ms`: the frame time at which the current hold ends without new matches (person: last match + `hold_s`, or `still_hold_s` for a stationary track; animal: last match + animal hold 6 s; vehicle: last match + `hold_s`, or `max_hold_s` for a parked track; the latest over the live tracks), `null` when idle. Also in `GET /v1/cameras/{camera}`.

## Second look: person and animal confirmation guard (0.3.2)

With `second_look` on (default), a person or animal detection at or above its start threshold (`start_thr` / `animal_start_thr`) that is not merely continuing an already CONFIRMED track is a mandatory second-look candidate (ahead of the borderline ones; the cap of 2 re-checks per frame and the 10 s reuse by object stay). It may count toward starting or confirming a track only if the re-check (or the reused result for that object) found the SAME group at a score >= `SECOND_LOOK['confirm_min'][group]` (person 0.40, animal 0.40). Otherwise (`not_found`, `not_upgraded`, `lowered` below that, or the cap prevented the check, then `second_look: "skipped"`) the detection is doubtful: it stays in the reply with its score and `second_look_doubt: true`, but the trackers see its score capped at `cont_thr + 0.05` (person) or `animal_cont_thr + 0.05` (animal), so it can still continue an already confirmed track but cannot start or confirm one. A confirmed track is never re-checked just for continuing. The doubt found on an earlier frame of the same object (for example the borderline 0.44 frame) is reused for its later, higher-scoring frames. Vehicles are unchanged (`not_found` keeps the detection). `rejected` still drops the detection from the trackers. `GET /v1/metrics` `second_look.skipped_60s` counts checks the cap prevented. With `second_look` off nothing is demoted.

## Review page: annotation, reviewed lists, dashboard (0.3.2)

- `GET /v1/journal?labelled=true|false&status=closed|ongoing` add to the list filters (400 for other values).
- `GET /v1/journal/next?after=<id>&camera=&outcome=` returns `{item, remaining}`: the OLDEST closed episode without a label (start order; with `after` only those starting after that episode, so a skipped event is not offered again until the client wraps around by calling without `after`), `remaining` = all matching unlabelled closed episodes. `item` carries `question`; an invalid `after` is 400, an unknown one is ignored. `GET /v1/journal/{id}` carries `question` too.
- `question` = `{key, text, group, others, yes, no}`: the ONE plain question the Validate tab asks, derived from outcome, kind and groups (the first group of the episode is asked about, the rest are listed in `others`; `group` is the one the page highlights, other boxes are dimmed): confirmed person "Is there a person?", confirmed vehicle "Is this a moving vehicle?", confirmed animal "Is there an animal?", vetoed camera trigger "Is the frame really empty?" (no group highlighted), unflagged person / vehicle / animal "Is there a person | a moving vehicle | an animal the camera missed?", anything else "Is this a real event?". `yes` / `no` are the labels a Yes / No answer stores: Yes = `correct`; No = `false_alarm`, except "really empty" where No = `missed`. After a No the page offers "What is it?" (person, vehicle, animal, nothing, other): a class different from the asked group stores `wrong_class`, always with the note `is: <choice>`.
- `GET /v1/journal/summary?from=&to=&camera=&tz=` (period on the episode start in epoch ms, `tz` = minutes east of UTC within +-840 for the day buckets) returns, besides `rows` (camera x group x outcome / label counts), `totals`, `cameras` (one row per camera), `groups` (one per group) and `days` (events per local day, ascending). Rows of `totals`/`cameras`/`groups`: `{events, reviewed, correct, false_alarm, missed, wrong_class, confirmed, vetoed, unflagged, other, precision}`; `precision` = correct / reviewed, `null` when nothing is reviewed; `confirmed`/`vetoed`/`other` count by outcome, `unflagged` counts kind `unflagged`. `days[]` = `{day: "YYYY-MM-DD", total, confirmed, vetoed, unflagged, other}`.
- The page has four tabs. Events: filter bar and cards. Validate: one question per event with big Yes / No buttons (keys Y / N, S or the right arrow = skip, left arrow = previous), the frames below with the relevant boxes highlighted, every answer is saved at once and the next unreviewed event is shown (the one after it is prefetched), "N left". Reviewed: "To review" and "Reviewed" lists with totals; opening an event lets you change its label. Summary: KPI tiles (events, reviewed %, precision, false alarms, missed) for 24 h / 7 d / 14 d and a camera filter, a sortable per-camera table (worst precision first; a click on a row opens Validate for that camera), a per-category table and a per-day stacked bar chart (inline SVG). Boxes are labelled `cls score` with a marker (check = second look confirmed, ? = doubt or not found, cross = rejected); colours person red, vehicle blue, animal green.

## 0.3.3 notes

- Journal noise: an `unflagged` episode that never confirmed (no confirmation in the timeline, outcome not `confirmed`, and not `released` with groups) is deleted instead of closed when it ends (`end` replies `status: "discarded"`), and such episodes already on disk are removed at startup and in maintenance regardless of their labels (the count is logged).
- Summary categories: `unflagged` = kind `unflagged` (independent of the outcome); `confirmed` = confirmed at some point (outcome `confirmed`, a timeline entry containing "confirm", or groups present with outcome `released`); `vetoed` = outcome vetoed; `other` = everything else (aborted, released without groups, parked, stale, ongoing). Every episode is in exactly one of them. Summary `cameras` rows carry `display_name`.
- Display names: `POST /v1/cameras/{camera}/config` accepts `name` (string, at most 64 characters; control characters and `< > " ' ` &` are removed, whitespace collapsed; `""` clears it) and returns it in `GET` too. Journal episodes store `display_name` at `start` (the camera's configured name, else the id without a trailing `_<digits>`); the review page shows it everywhere and falls back to the id without the `_<digits>` suffix.
- Version: `/v1/health` and the log report the real add-on version: `VERIFIER_VERSION`, else `BUILD_VERSION` (the Supervisor passes it as a build argument, the Dockerfile exports it), else the `config.yaml` copied into the image, else a constant.

## Load shedding (0.3.6)

A frame sent with `prio=low` (heartbeat / pixel gate) that reaches the Coral worker while at least `BUSY_DEPTH` (1, `defaults.py`) other frames wait in the queue is reduced: only its BASE pass(es) run (one pass, or the hybrid whole frame plus tiles for wide frames), the zoom-out pass and the second look are skipped, and the reply carries `reduced: true` (always present, `false` otherwise). Person and animal detections whose second look was skipped that way follow the existing doubt rule (`second_look: "skipped"`, `second_look_doubt: true`): they cannot start or confirm a track but may continue an already confirmed one, so a reduced frame can never be the one that confirms a person. A cached second-look result is still reused (no invoke). Normal-priority frames (camera-triggered episodes) always run everything, and a low-priority frame that finds the queue empty runs everything too. `GET /v1/metrics` `load` has `reduced_frames_60s` and `shed_passes_60s` (the zoom-out pass plus the second looks that did not run, at most 2 per frame).

Promotion (0.3.6): the reduction is decided AFTER the base pass, from the camera's session and the base detections. A low-priority frame on a busy service is still run in full (zoom-out if configured, second look) when its camera is near a status change: (1) the person or animal session is a `candidate`, or the base pass has a person/animal detection at or above its start threshold that would start or confirm a track; (2) a confirmed person/animal track whose `hold_until_ms` is within 2 s of the frame's time (release imminent); (3) a vehicle whose moving/still classification is pending (a non-parked track younger than 3 s, or a strong vehicle with no track yet). Such frames reply `reduced: false` and are counted in `GET /v1/metrics` `load.promoted_frames_60s`; `reduced_frames_60s` and `shed_passes_60s` count only the frames that stayed reduced.

## 0.3.7 notes

Stale episodes: an ongoing episode is closed as `stale` when the CLIENT has not sent `start` / `update` for 2 h. Linked frames no longer count as activity (a camera streams forever; the 0.3.6 behaviour kept episodes alive for as long as frames arrived). A confirmed stale episode is kept (closed, outcome `stale`), an unconfirmed `unflagged` one is removed. The maintenance runs on the journal worker at least every 5 minutes with or without traffic (`defaults.JOURNAL["maintain_idle_s"]`), and stale episodes never count against the ongoing caps (64 in total, 8 per camera): they are closed before the cap is checked.

## 0.3.10 notes

## 0.3.11 notes

The plugin publishes Coral-verified detections on the camera's ObjectDetector as `verified_person`, `verified_vehicle` and `verified_animal`, next to the camera's own AI classes, and no longer re-publishes the camera's raw events. The Scrypted server does not mask a device's property-less events behind a mixin that implements the same interface (masking is disabled in `server/src/state.ts`, `notifyInterfaceEventFromMixin`), so every listener receives both and tells them apart by class name. 0.3.10 tried to replace the raw classes and, in Off/Shadow, re-published raw events that consumers then received twice.

## 0.3.12 notes

- Fast person confirmation (per camera, `POST .../config` keys `fast_confirm` bool default false, `fast_n` int 2-10 default 3, `fast_window_ms` 100-5000 default 700, `fast_move` 0-5 default 0.15; constants in `defaults.TRACKER`). Besides the tick rule, a person is confirmed when one per-FRAME IoU track (IoU >= 0.3 between consecutive detections >= `start_thr`; frames with the same clamped `t` count once) has >= `fast_n` frames >= `start_thr` within `fast_window_ms` of frame time AND either its box centre moved >= `fast_move` x the box height between its first and last qualifying frame (static look-alikes never move) or the frame request carries `claim=person`. The tick track under that box is then confirmed at once; hold, stillness lock, max hold and release are unchanged. Second-look doubtful detections (score capped) never qualify. With `fast_confirm` false nothing changes.
- `POST .../frame` takes an optional `claim=person|vehicle|animal` (anything else 400): the camera's own AI claims that class for the current episode; only `person` is used (fast confirm).
- `person.confirmed_by` in replies and `GET /v1/cameras/{camera}`: `"fast_move" | "fast_claim" | "ticks"` for the current confirmation, `null` while not confirmed.
- Journal: `POST .../episodes` accepts `inconclusive` (bool: a veto resting on too little evidence; set on update/end, `false` clears it) and `detect` (list of person|vehicle|animal, the camera's enabled groups). A vetoed camera episode with `inconclusive` gets the question `{key: "inconclusive", text: "Was <a person or an animal ...> there?" (the `detect` groups, else "someone or something"), yes: "missed", no: "correct"}`; a well-founded veto keeps "Is the frame really empty?". Labels are unchanged.
- Journal merge: an `unflagged` episode whose `start` (t_ms) falls within `merge_after_veto_s` (5 s, 2 s clock skew tolerated) after the end of a closed VETOED camera episode of the same camera is merged into it: the camera episode is reopened (status ongoing, outcome cleared, `inconclusive` cleared, the previous verdict kept in `merged_from`, the unflagged id in `merged_ids`, a timeline entry "merged: unflagged episode after the veto"), and every later call with the unflagged id goes to it (the item stays kind `camera`; its outcome, groups and frames come from the merged part). Replies keep the caller's `id` and add `merged_into`. The alias survives a restart (rebuilt from `merged_ids`).

## 0.3.13 notes

Frame replies carry `person.fast` while fast confirm is on: `{hi, n, moved}` = person boxes at or above `start_thr` in this frame, frames of the best fast track within `fast_window_ms`, and that track's centre shift in box heights. ## 0.3.14 notes

Fast confirm retuned on real walks (0.3.13 diagnostics: strong frames are sparse while a person enters, the best track held 2 strong frames in 0.7 s, and walking moved the box 0.04-0.07 box heights between frames): `fast_n` 2, `fast_window_ms` 1000, `fast_move` 0.03, the two qualifying frames at least 200 ms apart (`FAST_MIN_SPAN_MS`), and movement is the larger of the centre shift and the change in box height (walking towards or away from the camera). A static box still never fast-confirms without `claim=person`.
